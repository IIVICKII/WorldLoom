TIER: Tablet
POV: first person past

1. Working Title, Tags & Logline
Working Title: The Quiet Ledger
Genres: cozy fantasy, slow-burn romance, light political intrigue
Logline: A bookkeeper hired to audit a merchant house's accounts finds its new head reading every contract twice, laughing at the wrong moments, and hoping nobody notices how lost he is. She notices. The season's contract renewals are three weeks away.

2. The World
The town of Aldwick sits at the mouth of a slow river, three days from the capital by cart and one by river-barge if the current is kind. Trade runs through it rather than to it, which has always suited the townsfolk. Mornings smell of tar and bread from the harbour bakeries; afternoons belong to the warehouse bells.
Magic is a licensed trade skill, not a birthright. Anyone can learn cantrips at the guild hall for a fee, and most people know two or three practical ones (fire-lighting, stain-lifting, weather-reading) the way they know a trade. Standing in Aldwick runs on land, contracts, and coin.
House Varlen
Narrative Weight: Supporting
The wealthiest merchant house in Aldwick, grand and slightly threadbare: good rugs over cold floors, a counting room that smells of dust and lamp oil, a portrait gallery with one empty hook. It holds the river warehouse contracts every other trader depends on.
Aldwick Trade Council
Narrative Weight: Background
The trading families' council; it meets each autumn to renew the warehouse contracts, usually a formality.
Guild Hall
Narrative Weight: Background
Where anyone can pay to learn practical cantrips.
Hard Rules:
- Magic is learned, not innate; anyone can study it at the guild hall for a fee.
- No spell can alter memory, compel belief, or force loyalty.
- House Varlen's warehouse contracts are renewed by the trade council each autumn.
- Losing the contracts would cripple half the trading families in Aldwick.

3. Main Cast
Player Character: Mira Fenlow

Mira Fenlow
Narrative Weight: Core
Age: twenty-six
Gender: female (she/her)
Occupation: bookkeeper, hired to audit House Varlen's books
Appearance: Five foot four, sturdy and square-shouldered from years of hauling her father's stock crates. Round face, freckles that darken in summer, a small chip in one front tooth she stopped hiding at nineteen. Thick brown hair pinned in a knot that slips by noon; she shoves the pins back without looking. Hazel eyes. Ink on the side of her right hand most days. Grey wool work dress, sleeves rolled to the elbow, a leather satchel worn pale at the strap. Sits very straight at a desk and slouches everywhere else; taps a pencil against her teeth while thinking.
Personality: careful, observant, wry, quietly proud of good work, unsure it counts, notices what doesn't match
Background: Kept her father's shop accounts before he could afford a clerk, and never stopped. Five years of small, safe ledgers for small, safe shops.
Wants: to be trusted with something that matters.
Fears: that carefulness is the only interesting thing about her.
Reflex under pressure: goes still and asks one plain question.
Source of friction: she has never been handed real responsibility and isn't sure she can carry it; insecurity, not a defect.
Relationships at start: hired by Hollis Bray, whom she has met twice and likes; knows Josselin Varlen and Perrin Ashgrove only by reputation.
Voice: Default register is warm, dry, and quick: short plain sentences, contractions, and she often answers a question with a smaller, sharper one. Habit: says "Right," on its own to buy a second before answering something hard, at most once a scene. Flattered, she jokes about her handwriting. When something is truly wrong she drops to very few words and stops joking. Talks ledgers only at the desk. Never says variance, liability, reconcile, or projection away from the ledgers, and never sounds like a clerk reading a report.
Sample line: "You've got three different inks in this book. Did somebody run out, or did somebody hide something?"
Pull: toward being trusted, and whether she takes it is play's business.

Josselin Varlen
Narrative Weight: Core
Age: twenty-nine
Gender: male (he/him)
Occupation: head of House Varlen for eight months; formerly a river-barge clerk
Appearance: Six foot one, long-limbed, moves like someone expecting to bump into doorframes. Narrow pale face, a nose broken once and set slightly crooked, a quick lopsided grin; his ears flush red first. Black hair worn too long, curling in the rain. Grey eyes. A thin white fishing-hook scar across the back of his left hand. Wears his cousin's good coats, a fraction short in the sleeve, over plain shirts of his own. Clasps his hands behind his back to look like a head of house; fidgets with his cuffs when he forgets.
Personality: kind, quick to laugh, hides worry behind jokes, doubts his right to the chair
Background: Inherited the house by distance of blood when his cousin left abruptly; has read the current contracts more than once and understood less than he lets on.
Wants: not to be found out before he finds his footing.
Fears: that everyone already suspects, and is only waiting to watch him fail.
Reflex under pressure: makes a joke to buy a second to think.
Source of friction: out of his depth and hiding it; he doubts his right to the chair where Mira doubts her worth.
Relationships at start: leans on Hollis Bray and half fears disappointing him; has dined with Perrin Ashgrove twice and liked him.
Voice: Default register is easy, chatty, and self-deprecating: longer sentences that wander and land on a joke, lots of "honestly" and "look". Habit: answers a formal question with a joke first and the real answer second, at most twice a scene. Called "Master Varlen", he goes briefly stiff and over-polite, then laughs at himself. Truly afraid, the jokes stop and he goes quiet and plain. Never uses contract language he doesn't understand (heretofore, pursuant, the party of the first part), and never sounds like the cousin whose coats he wears.
Sample line: "Honestly, I've read this clause four times and I think it's about barrels. Look, it might be about barrels."
Pull: toward growing into the chair or admitting he never wanted it; neither is decided.

4. Supporting Cast & Antagonists

Hollis Bray
Narrative Weight: Supporting
Age: sixty-one
Gender: male (he/him)
Occupation: steward of House Varlen for thirty years
Look: grey and unhurried, a ring of every key in the house
Want: to see the house survive the transition.
Function: the reason Mira is at House Varlen; keeps the house running and knows where every key is.
Texture: kind, patient, a little deaf in one ear, loyal to the house more than to any one Varlen. No flaw: the story is better for one person who is simply good at their job.
Voice: Default register is slow, warm, and old-fashioned ("young sir", "if you please"). Habit: tells a short story about the house's past instead of answering directly, about once a scene. Worried, he stops telling stories. Never gossips about the absent cousin.
Sample line: "Mind the third stair, Miss Fenlow. It's been loose since the old master's day, and nobody's had the heart to fix it."

Perrin Ashgrove
Narrative Weight: Supporting
Role: Rival
Age: forty-four
Gender: male (he/him)
Occupation: owner of Aldwick's second-largest warehouse
Look: neat, well-dressed, an easy smile
Want: the river contracts, fairly won if possible.
Function: drives the contract pressure; works through dinners, favours, and a word in a councillor's ear, with good relationships across half the council and room to spare in his warehouse.
Texture: patient, courteous, sharp with numbers, pays on time; not wrong that the town might be better served by competition.
Voice: Default register is genial and unhurried, compliments first and the point second. Habit: calls people by their trade when friendly. Challenged, he turns very polite and very short. Never threatens anyone, and never says anything he couldn't repeat before the council.
Sample line: "Our bookkeeper! I'd heard Varlen had found one. You'll stay for a cup, won't you?"
If unopposed: Perrin persuades enough client families and councillors that the council reopens the contracts for bidding at the autumn meeting, and wins them.
(Internal pacing note — do not carry into lore-facing text downstream.)

No antagonist. Opposition in this story comes from Perrin's fair rivalry, the three-week deadline, the unexplained irregularity in the books, and Mira's and Josselin's private doubts.

5. World State & Dramatic Situation
Status Quo: Mira arrives at House Varlen this morning to begin auditing the books. Josselin is in the counting room reading a contract for the third time when Hollis brings her in.
Recent Events & Temporary Conditions:
- Last week Hollis Bray hired Mira without telling Josselin, because something in the books felt wrong to him; Josselin has not been asked.
- This month Perrin Ashgrove began asking House Varlen's client families, politely, whether they are happy with their service.
Known Upcoming:
- In three weeks the trade council meets to renew the river warehouse contracts.
Active Pressures:
- The council meeting (Known Upcoming). If left unaddressed, this moves toward Josselin standing before the council with books he cannot explain.
- Perrin's quiet courtship of the client families. If left unaddressed, this moves toward the house's oldest client, the wool shippers, telling Josselin kindly at a dinner that they are thinking of moving their goods to Ashgrove's warehouse.
- The irregularity in the books. If left unaddressed, this moves toward Mira finding a second, larger gap that is hard to call a clerical error.
Possible Directions:
- The audit finds an honest mistake, Josselin learns the business properly, and the house is renewed on merit.
- The audit finds something the absent cousin did, and Josselin has to decide whether to tell the council.
- Mira and Josselin's working relationship becomes something warmer at whatever pace play finds, or stays a friendship, or stays professional.
- Perrin wins the contracts fairly, and the story becomes about what House Varlen is without them.
Open Questions & Secrets:
- Why Josselin's cousin left, and whether the irregularity has anything to do with it — unresolved-by-design.
- Whether the gap in the books is a mistake or a theft — unresolved-by-design.
- Hollis knows the cousin left in the middle of the night with one trunk. He has not told Josselin.

6. Core Memory & Tone
Core Memory Candidates:
- Aldwick is a slow-river trading town; trade runs through it.
- Magic in Aldwick is a learned trade skill, sold at the guild hall.
- No spell can alter memory, compel belief, or force loyalty.
- House Varlen holds the river warehouse contracts.
- The trade council renews the contracts each autumn.
- Half of Aldwick's trading families depend on those contracts.
Tone & Atmosphere: warm domestic detail; ink and ledger dust; rain on old windows; comedy from mismatched expectations, never slapstick; romance that builds through small kindnesses noticed but not named. Themes: competence being trusted; two people each privately sure they are the only one out of their depth; stakes that are real but human-scale.

7. Generation Notes
Genre Contract:
Primary genre: cozy fantasy, with slow-burn romance and light intrigue as modifiers
Stakes scale: local
Opposition model: friction, circumstance, internal, rival; antagonist floor: 0
Flaw model: insecurities for Main Cast; none required for supporting characters
Pressure tempo: gentle, on a three-week calendar
Darkness ceiling: no on-page death, no cruelty played straight, no violence
Engine: competence being noticed, small kindnesses, the pleasure of a problem slowly coming clear
Tag Conflict Resolutions: cozy and political intrigue pull against each other; per the blending rule the intrigue shrinks to fit the cozy tone, so the opposition is one fair rival and a deadline rather than a conspiracy.
Consistency Fixes: None.
Starting Point: chosen by Stage 1, as no START was given — the morning Mira arrives, before Josselin knows she has been hired, so the player makes the first real decision about how they meet. Nothing relocated.
Cast Sizing Decisions: Player Character Mira Fenlow, as the lead the premise is told through. Hollis Bray kept: he is the reason Mira is here at all. Perrin Ashgrove kept: he drives the contract pressure; no lead or circumstance can want the contracts for themselves. No other characters invented. Two Main Cast by the premise's own framing. Supporting: two, below the top of the band. Antagonists: zero, at the contract's floor. Cast fit the tier band; no resizing needed.
